# Checklist for managing the risks of heat in the workplace

Publisher: Safe Work Australia
Authority in Victoria: National guidance, not law in Victoria
Source: https://www.safeworkaustralia.gov.au/safety-topic/hazards/working-heat/checklist-managing-risks-heat-workplace
Accessed: 2026-09-25

---

Use this checklist if you work in or near heat.


| **Question about risk**  | **Why this is important**  | 
| Is the environment hot or is there fire at the workplace? | This contributes to incidents such as heat-related illness and burns. | 
| Are days and nights hotter than usual? | A heatwave can make it harder to sleep and workers may become fatigued. | 
| Is it humid? | High humidity makes it harder for the body to cool itself. | 
| Do people work during the hot part of the day or year? | Scheduling work at certain times can increase or decrease risk. | 
| How often can workers take breaks somewhere cool? | Working in heat for long periods of time is very dangerous. | 
| Is there air movement or a breeze? | This can help cool workers. | 
| Is the work intense or long? | The harder the body is working the more heat it needs to lose. | 
| Are workers physically fit and acclimatised? | Fit and acclimatised workers can generally tolerate heat better. | 
| Do workers wear hot clothing (including PPE)? | Some clothing can prevent air from circulating or sweat from evaporating. | 
| Are the workers qualified, trained and experienced? | Experienced workers may be more efficient and use less energy for the same work. They may also be more aware of the hazards, health effects and controls. | 
| Do workers have medical conditions? | Some conditions and medications can make workers less able to cope with heat. | 
| Is there cool drinking water or electrolyte drinks on hand? | Dehydration can be dangerous and contributes to heat-related illness. |
